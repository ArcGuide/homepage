Main program

One-Person Company cohort

Eight weeks to build a small real product with AI, launch it, find real users and write down honestly how it went. Shipping, not simulating.

How to applyThe 8 weeks
12students per cohort
8weeks, about 10 hours a week
1build log every week
1live product at Demo Day

Who it's for

  • University students (undergraduate or master's) in mainland China. This is cohort one; other regions come later.
  • Any major. You don't need to know how to code, but you need to be willing to learn by doing.
  • About 10 hours a week, for 8 weeks. If you can't commit that, please wait for a later cohort; it's fairer to the other applicants.

What you leave with

  • A live product at a public link.
  • Real usage data, even if the numbers are small.
  • Eight honest build logs and a case study.

No certificate. The link to your product, data and logs is the proof.

The 8 weeks

One concrete deliverable every week

Each deliverable goes into that week's build log.

WeekFocusDeliverable
0Set upAI coding assistant, git and a deploy platform working; one line of code live on the internet; 3 candidate problems
1Find the problemTalk to at least 10 potential users; pick one problem; one-page problem statement
2Smallest versionv0 live at a public URL
3First usersAt least 5 real users have tried it; notes on how they used it and where they got stuck
4Iterate + midpointFix the biggest problem; 5-minute demo at group review
5DistributionOne channel experiment (Xiaohongshu, Zhihu, a community…) with numbers
6Retention or revenueMeasure whether users come back, or try charging, even ¥1
7PolishReliability, privacy, onboarding; write the case study
8Demo DayPublic showcase: live product, real numbers, full build log
Weekly rhythm

Mostly async, by design

  1. Sunday: build log due

    What shipped, numbers, what users said, where you're stuck, next step, and how you used AI.

  2. Monday: digest

    An AI summary of all 12 logs, so the reviewer can focus on judgment.

  3. Wednesday: 1-hour group review

    3–4 projects in depth each week. Everyone else gets written feedback. AI-drafted feedback is labeled as such, and a person reads it before it's sent.

  4. Anytime: peer pairs

    You and a partner test each other's products and read each other's logs.

Graduation

Two outcomes, both public

Shipped

Product live at Demo Day. At least 10 real users who aren't classmates, friends or family, backed by screenshots, analytics or an anonymized list. All 8 logs. A public case study.

Completed

Full participation and all 8 logs, but fewer users. Your case study explains why it didn't work and what you learned. That's valuable too.

Honesty clause

“Nobody used it” beats a made-up number

Faked data or someone else's work ends your participation. Logs need evidence for their numbers, and peer pairs test each other's products. We'd much rather read an honest log about a flop than a polished one about nothing.

Your build logs

Private by default, public by choice

Your logs are visible only to the cohort. Each one is published only if you agree, with your name or anonymously, and you can withdraw it later.

Published logs and case studies are how future applicants, employers and admissions readers can see what the cohort really did.

How to apply

Show us you can build, and that you'll tell the truth

  1. The basics

    Who you are and how much time you really have.

  2. Something you've made

    Code, writing, a video, a club, a small business, anything. If there's nothing yet, one page on a problem you want to solve and how you'd test it.

  3. The 48-hour task

    Use any AI tool to build a one-page tool that anyone can open, plus a note of up to 300 words on what went wrong and how you fixed it. Rough is fine; we want to see that you can ship and record it honestly.

Reviewed with school names removed. We look at three things: did you ship, is your account honest, and is it a real problem for real people.
