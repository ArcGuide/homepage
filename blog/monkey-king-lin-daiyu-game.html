Monkey King and Lin Daiyu as a couple: what's the pairing, and is there a game?

Why fans paired the Monkey King with Lin Daiyu from Dream of the Red Chamber, and a free browser game where you plan the couple's days.

STARC Institute · 2026-10-23

Short answer

Yes. There is a free browser game where the Monkey King from Journey to the West and Lin Daiyu from Dream of the Red Chamber are already a couple and you plan their days. It runs on a phone with no download, no sign-up and no purchases. The pairing itself grew out of AI-made fan videos that put two classic characters who never meet into the same modern scenes.

Is there a Monkey King and Lin Daiyu game?

Yes. We built a free browser game where the Monkey King and Lin Daiyu are already a couple, and you plan their days together. It runs in your phone's browser: no download, no sign-up, no purchases.

Play it: starc.institute/games/shengdai/. The game text is currently in Chinese, but most of it is pictures and taps.

If you've seen the pairing in your feed and wondered why everyone is shipping a monkey and a poet, here is the background, and what the game does differently from the videos.

Who these two are

They come from two of the best-known classic novels in East Asian literature, and the books never cross.

The Monkey King, Sun Wukong, is the hero of Journey to the West. According to the Wikipedia article on Sun Wukong, he is born from a magical stone on the Mountain of Flowers and Fruit. He learns the 72 transformations, can travel 108,000 li in a single somersault, and can turn strands of his hair into copies of himself. He rebels against heaven, is imprisoned under a mountain for five hundred years, and is finally released to guard a monk on a long pilgrimage, wearing a circlet that tightens whenever the monk chants a certain sutra. He is loud, fearless and impossible to keep still.

Lin Daiyu is the heroine of Dream of the Red Chamber. The Wikipedia article on Lin Daiyu describes her as well educated, witty and beautiful but physically frail; melancholic, hypersensitive and an instinctive poet, with a sharp tongue her maids learn to tiptoe around. In the novel's framing story she began life as a crimson flower that a divine attendant watered, and she vowed to repay him with a lifetime of tears.

Put them side by side and you have the oldest setup in romantic comedy: the restless one and the quiet one. He can't sit through a poem; she can't stand a mess. He would solve any problem by punching it or flying over it; she would rather write about it.

Where the pairing came from

The pairing grew out of fan videos and images made with AI tools that put the two characters into the same modern scenes: shopping, eating, going to the movies, sheltering from the rain. This October it spread widely again, and those clips were reported to have racked up billions of views.

Part of the appeal is simply the contrast. Part of it, as one widely shared newspaper commentary put it, is that fans aren't only shipping a romance. They're also enjoying two different ways of living: one character who fights his way through everything and never accepts his fate, and one who feels everything and refuses to pretend otherwise.

The same commentary praised the creativity and also asked creators to respect legal and ethical limits, especially around the original works' spirit, the characters' images and the actors who played them in well-known TV adaptations. We took that seriously when we built the game.

What the game does differently

A video is something you watch. In the game, you decide.

Five hundred years later, in an old riverside city, the Monkey King and Lin Daiyu already live together. Each day you pick what they do:

  • Together: walk by the lake, cook, go to the movies, drink tea and listen to the rain, try yoga. Shared moments are the sweetest.
  • Apart: he delivers food, hauls bricks on a building site and works as a tour guide on a monkey hill; she runs a small bookshop and submits poems.
  • Quarrels and making up: he wants noise, she wants quiet. Push one of them too often and they'll argue, and making up is usually the best scene of the day.
  • Magic slips out: he delivers takeout by cloud, cooks with fire he shouldn't have, or pulls out a hair to make ten copies of himself to sell peaches. Sometimes it helps. Sometimes it doesn't.
  • Real calendar: the game follows today's date, with limited stories and gallery pictures in the week around holidays. When the couple runs out of energy, they rest, and you come back in a few hours.
  • Gallery: every sweet moment is saved as an illustration you can open full screen and save to your phone.

There's no ending. Their days just keep going, and new gallery pictures keep being added.

Two rules we set for ourselves: no TV stills and no actors' faces, since the novels are centuries old but the adaptations and their actors are not ours to use; and no rewriting of the original plots. Everything in the game is new, everyday life.

Who made it

The game was built and launched with AI by STARC, a small nonprofit, as a demo for its one-person company cohort. The cohort asks students to spend 8 weeks building a real small product with AI, launch it, find real users and write down honestly what happened. No coding experience is required.

We held ourselves to the same standard: no hand-written code, five days from idea to launch. What broke, especially in the art, and how we run the game with no server, is in our build log: Can one person with no coding experience ship a web game with AI in a week?

The short version

The Monkey King and Lin Daiyu pairing is a fan crossover of two classic novels, made popular by AI-generated videos, and the contrast between a restless hero and a quiet poet is the whole charm. If you'd rather plan their days than watch them, play the free browser game. It runs on your phone with no download and no sign-up.

Related questions

Who are the Monkey King and Lin Daiyu?

The Monkey King, Sun Wukong, is the hero of the classic novel Journey to the West: born from a stone, master of 72 transformations, able to cover 108,000 li in one somersault. Lin Daiyu is the heroine of Dream of the Red Chamber: a gifted, sensitive poet. The two books never put them in the same story.

Is there a Monkey King and Lin Daiyu game I can play?

Yes. It's a free browser game at starc.institute/games/shengdai/. Open it on your phone or laptop; there's nothing to download and no account to make. The game text is currently in Chinese.

Does the game cost anything, and where is my progress saved?

It's free with no in-app purchases. Your save lives in your own browser, so clearing site data or switching browsers loses it. The game has a backup code you can copy before switching devices.

Does the game use footage or actors from TV adaptations?

No. The characters are AI-generated designs, not stills or actors' faces, and the stories are original everyday scenes rather than rewrites of the novels.
